In the past, we've used a variety of typefaces across each page. Our effort to simplify — not just branding but the entire user experience — has led us to pare down the types we use. In losing the formal serif style, our brand becomes much more approachable, modern, and youthful.

Photography
Real moments, real passion, real people. Our photography should evoke emotion and provoke engagement. While composition, subject matter,  and diversity are important, the feelings our images stir are essential. Our bullseye is photography which captures the heart and soul of our subjects.
Environments
Environments tell the story of what our customers build. Choose real workplaces that reflect the breadth of businesses running on our platform, from a three-person agency to a global engineering floor.
Look For:
Real working spaces such as offices, studios, retail back rooms, co-working spaces, home offices, labs and operations centers. A mix of scale and industry across multiple business verticals Bright interiors with natural light, clean lines and depth Neutral or naturally cool tones (white walls, wood, glass, slate) that harmonize with our blues
Avoid:
Server rooms with glowing blue lights, abstract "data" imagery and sci-fi tech clichés. Spaces that look like stock sets: empty glass boardrooms, staged desks, unused props. Visible competitor branding, third-party logos, readable confidential screens or documents. Strong color casts (heavy red, orange or magenta lighting)

Lighting and Composition
Light should feel like it belongs to the place: bright, warm and believable, as if the subject chose a good moment in their own day.
Natural window light or open shade, with soft fill matched to the ambient color Warm-neutral white balance (roughly 4800–5600K) and true skin tones Generous negative space on at least one side Subject on a thirds line, with eyes in the upper third Shallow-to-moderate depth  of field (around f/2.8–f/5.6) Camera at or slightly below eye level Calm backgrounds that do not distract from the subject matter
Hard on-camera flash, heavy contrast and moody low-key looks Faces in silhouette Oversaturated or heavily graded color Skin that reads orange or gray Shooting down on subjects from above
Model Diversity
Our customers are developers, founders, marketers, creatives, IT leads and operators in every part of the world. Casting should reflect that range naturally across a shoot and across the library, not by arranging it within a single frame. For shoots and stock selection, lean into diversity.
Range across ethnicity and skin tone, age, gender identity and expression, body type, height and ability (including assistive technology), style of dress and seniority Real customers and employees first; talent or stock who could credibly do the job shown Every demographic in leading, expert and decision-making roles Individual styling and grooming
Token compositions, with one of each demographic lined up in the frame Underrepresented groups only in supporting or background roles Stereotyped pairings of person and role Matching wardrobes Retouching that alters skin tone, body shape or features
People in Environments
These images place our customers at the center of the businesses they've built. The frame should say "this is theirs" before any copy does.
Subjects positioned as owners of their space: at their office, at their storefront, on their floor, at their workbench Clear, uncluttered space on at least one side, plus headroom for wide-to-vertical crops Environment adding context in the mid- and background, softened behind the subject One clear hero in group shots, led by focus, light, position or a speaking role Small, believable groups of 2–5 people engaged with the hero or the task
Arms-crossed power poses, high fives and staged handshakes Everyone smiling at camera at once Subjects pressed against frame edges Backgrounds busy enough to compete with the hero subject
People With Devices
The person is the story; the device is how they get it done. Show customers absorbed in meaningful work: launching a site, checking performance, collaborating on a fix.
Faces and body language prioritized over the screen Focus, satisfaction or quiet confidence Real devices in a realistic mix of brands and ages Natural interactions: typing, scrolling, pointing a colleague to a screen, glancing up mid-task Screens visible but not readable, flat and evenly lit for UI placement in post In groups, one person driving the moment while others lean in
Pointing and smiling at blank screens, holograms or floating icons Real customer data or competitor products on screen Logos on device lids Glare, screen moiré, and/or blue screen glow on faces Excessive device stacks (phone, tablet and laptop shown all at once) Copy space placed across the device

Event/On-Location Photography
Event coverage should make viewers feel they missed something worthwhile. Document the energy of real exchange: customers learning, sharing and connecting, represented as the people actually in attendance.
Speakers in action: gesturing, engaging the room, mid-sentence Audience reactions focused on one engaged face rather than rows of attendees Hallway and booth moments: demos, introductions, small-group conversations Environmental wides with brand presence visible but not dominant Details: badges, swag, stage set, hands on keyboards Range across the crowd, without staging it
Empty seats, people on phones, eating and backs of heads as hero images Unflattering mid-speech expressions Magenta or green stage casts Direct flash Interrupting a session for a photo
Company Headshots
Headshots should look like the person on their best ordinary day: approachable, confident and recognizable on a video call. One consistent setup across all employees keeps leadership pages and directories unified.
A soft, out-of-focus office or a light gray or white seamless backdrop Soft, broad key light with warm-neutral white balance and a subtle catchlight Eye-level camera with an  85–105mm equivalent lens Mid-chest-up framing with generous space above and on both sides Everyday work wardrobe in solid mid-tones; palette-complementary tones encouraged but optional Glasses on if worn daily,  angled to reduce glare
Dramatic shadows Tight crops that limit avatar and tile use Fine patterns and logos Retouching beyond temporary blemishes, stray hairs and lint (leadership portraits may be refined further at the leader's discretion) Altering face or body shape
Event/Speaker Headshots
Speaker headshots arrive from many sources and are styled to each event's brand, so they must look like they belong to one lineup. The goal is consistency of feel, not identical photos.
A standardized light gray or white backdrop when we shoot Lighting matched to Company Headshots (when possible) Two framings when we shoot:  a mid-chest headshot and a looser half-body frame with room for session titles Supplied photos that are recent (within 2–3 years), at least 1500px on the short edge, evenly lit, with eyes to camera Consistent crop, head size and eye line across the lineup
Mixing heavily retouched studio portraits with casual snapshots Group crops, sunglasses or heavy filters Stylizing or recoloring Duotone or brand-color treatments that obscure skin tone Tight cropping or photos without additional space in frame
Approach
How we work on set and in post, across every type of shoot.
Directing
Direct with activities, not poses: "walk Sam through the new release" rather than "smile at each other." Shoot continuously through conversations to catch laughter, nods and thinking. Use light direction (positioning, speaking roles) to establish a hero in group shots.
Coverage
Capture wide, medium and detail frames from every setup, in both horizontal and vertical orientation. Shoot each environment with and without people; empty plates give designers flexible backgrounds. Look for leading lines, doorways and windows that create natural copy space.
Casting & Lighting
Rotate who plays the hero, and track casting across shoots so the library stays balanced. Light and expose for every skin tone in the frame; adjust when different tones share a scene.
On Location and At Events
Use fast lenses and higher ISO before flash; bounce flash when needed. Frame key moments loosely to preserve crop and copy space. Confirm photo-release signage, honor opt-out indicators such as colored lanyards, and ask before close portraits.
Headshot Sessions
Allow 3–5 minutes per person and shoot conversationally. Capture a few expressions, from a relaxed closed-mouth smile to an open smile, and let people choose.
Post-Production
Apply one consistent, light grade per shoot with realistic saturation. Gently balance white balance and exposure across headshot lineups. Designers mask and retouch supplied speaker headshots to fit the event brand, keeping edges, including hair, natural.
